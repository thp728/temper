Explain the tradeoffs between Raft and Paxos in high-latency wide area networks (WANs). Focus specifically on multi-datacenter consensus latency, pipelined log replication, leader failover recovery time, and whether Multi-Paxos or EPaxos provides real-world write latency advantages over standard single-leader Raft.
Assistant Response
186 tokens · Validated

When evaluating consensus protocols across high-latency WAN topologies (e.g. RTT > 80ms across regions), the primary performance bottleneck is cross-region round trips required for state machine commitment.

1. Leader Bottleneck & Latency: Standard Raft forces all state updates through the elected leader. If clients are co-located in Region A but the Raft leader resides in Region B, every write incurs at least 2 WAN RTTs before acknowledgment.

2. Multi-Paxos vs. Raft: While Multi-Paxos allows leases and decoupled role assignment, practically both achieve consensus in 1 WAN RTT from the leader in steady state. EPaxos (Egalitarian Paxos) eliminates dedicated leaders entirely, allowing any replica to propose writes in 1 RTT when concurrent conflict probability is low (<2%).
